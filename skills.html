    A NOTE ON AI

    You're looking at the proof.

    I can read R output myself — the syntax, the coefficients, what a p-value is and isn't telling you. AI didn't replace that; it changed how fast I can move once I already know what I'm looking at. I know enough to prompt precisely, catch when an output looks wrong, and work through the rest myself instead of stalling. That's the transferable part — knowing when a problem needs a machine, when it needs a person, and being resourceful enough either way.

    This site is the same instinct applied somewhere I had less built-in fluency. I directed the build end to end — described the design, caught when the copy read like a robot had written it, pushed back on layouts that didn't work, and knew when a piece of research was being oversimplified. I didn't need to know JavaScript already. I needed to know what good looked like and how to get there.

    It's the same thing I'd bring into a room with a data scientist and a room with a C-suite — reading a model's output well enough to know what survives contact with a real business decision, and moving fast enough with AI to get there without waiting on someone else to translate it for me.

    This website is that argument, made literal.
